\section{Implementation and Evaluation}
\label{sec:benchmark}

For an empirical evaluation, we implemented all three of our modulus-conversion protocols by realizing $\func_\abb$ for a dishonest majority, using the SPDZ2k protocol~\cite{C:CDESX18}, i.e., authenticated additive secret sharing over $\ints_{2^\kappa}$.
This places our numbers under the same security model as the two baselines we compare against: Noah's Ark~\cite{EPRINT:DDEMOR23} and Fhenix~\cite{CCS:ZZLP25} are both dishonest-majority protocols, and instantiating $\func_\abb$ with an honest-majority scheme would understate our costs.
Following Fhenix, we implemented the offline and online parts of our protocols separately:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Offline phase.}
        This is implemented using the MP-SPDZ library~\cite{EPRINT:Keller20}.
        In this phase, all correlated randomness specific to a modulus conversion is prepared: the shared mask $\shared{m}_{q'}$ and the correction table $\shared{u_0}_q, \dots, \shared{u_{\beta-1}}_q$ for $\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv1}$ (\cref{protocol:modconv1-preproc}) and $\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv2}$ (\cref{protocol:modconv2-preproc}), and the shared mask together with its bit decomposition for $\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv3}$ (\cref{protocol:modconv3-preproc}).
    \item \emph{Online phase.}
        This is likewise implemented in MP-SPDZ, so that both phases are measured by the same instrumentation and under the same wire protocol.
        Mirroring Fhenix's design, where precomputed Sign and ModLTZ gates are loaded into memory at the start, the preprocessed values are handed to the parties by a trusted dealer at the beginning of the run and the corresponding cost is excluded from the reported online figures; only the conversion itself and the final opening are measured.
\end{enumerate}

All benchmarks are run on a single machine, with the \texttt{tc} utility used to emulate network conditions and constraints for both phases.
This utility allows precise control over network behavior, such as introducing configurable delays and bandwidth limitations.
As in Fhenix, the emulated network has a fully connected topology of nodes using TCP for pairwise communication; concretely, we pass MP-SPDZ's \texttt{-d} (direct communication) flag, so that parties communicate pairwise rather than relaying through a coordinator, matching that topology.

To compare directly with Fhenix, and through it with the state of the art~\cite{EPRINT:DDEMOR23}, we use exactly the same \gls{lwe} parameters $(n, q, p) = (1024, 2^{64}, 2)$, hence $k = 64$, $m = 1$, $l = 63$, which are also commonly used for TFHE~\cite{JC:CGGI20} in commercial implementations.
We evaluate the same decryption step Fhenix benchmarks: given a threshold-shared \gls{lwe} ciphertext, the shared noisy inner product $\shared{z}_q = \shared{\langle c, s \rangle}_q$ satisfies $z = \mu \cdot (q/p) + e$, and recovering $\mu$ amounts to removing the noise term $e$.
Where Fhenix removes it with a small number of preprocessed comparison-type gates, we obtain shares of $e$ modulo a small $q' \mid q$ for free via $\DivModConv$, convert them back up to the ciphertext modulus $q$ with one of our protocols, and subtract.
Since the noise term is bounded by construction (it lies in a public interval fixed by the scheme's parameters), the precondition of $\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv1}^{q,t,\beta}$ (\cref{protocol:modconv1}) and of $\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv2}^{q,t,\beta}$ (\cref{protocol:modconv2}) is met without any extra assumption; we run $\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv3}^{q,t}$ (\cref{protocol:modconv3}) as well, as a second data point covering settings where that bound is inconvenient to guarantee.
We use the same party counts $N \in \{4, 8, 16\}$ as Fhenix, and cross the same ping times $\{1, 10, 100\}\,\mathrm{ms}$ with the same bandwidths $\{100\,\mathrm{Mbit/s}, 1\,\mathrm{Gbit/s}\}$.

The online phase of each protocol admits an exact round count, which we state here since it is independent of the machine and of MP-SPDZ's instrumentation.
$\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv1}$ and $\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv2}$ perform a single public opening for the conversion itself, plus the one opening that reveals the reconstructed plaintext, for two sequential openings in total and no secure multiplications at all.
$\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv3}$ performs one additional opening inside its masked comparison, for three in total.
For reference, Fhenix's online phase performs three sequential openings, plus two further rounds of authenticity checks in their implementation.

\subsection{Preprocessing}
\label{sec:benchmark-preproc}

To empirically benchmark our preprocessing protocols, we implemented them in MP-SPDZ~\cite{EPRINT:Keller20} on top of its SPDZ2k implementation.
As Fhenix does, we separate out the cost to generate shared random bits and multiplication triples, since there are many different ways to do this, and we run these tests under a single fixed network condition, $1$~ms ping time and $1$~Gbit/sec bandwidth.
Unlike Fhenix, whose preprocessing benchmark is reported for the MP-SPDZ default of two parties, our preprocessing cost depends on $N$ for $\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv2}$ (whose masks are per-party), so we report $N \in \{2, 4, 8, 16\}$; the $N = 2$ row is the one directly comparable to \cite[Table~5]{CCS:ZZLP25}.
\Cref{tab:benchmark-preproc} shows the cost to produce the preprocessing material for one modulus conversion, normalized per thousand decryptions to match Fhenix's reporting.

\paragraph{Triple-generation communication.}
Our preprocessing protocols use secure multiplications and random-bit generation via $\func_\abb$.
For $\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv1}$ and $\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv3}$ with $q$ and $t$ both powers of two, the preprocessing uses only shared random bits and no secure multiplications at all: the $\beta = 2$ specialization of \cref{protocol:modconv1-preproc} obtains its correction table from a single random bit and local linear combinations, and \cref{protocol:modconv3-preproc} needs only the bits of the mask.
$\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv2}$ is the exception, since its per-party masks must be combined, and its multiplication count grows with $N$ as reported in \cref{tab:benchmark-preproc}.
In SPDZ2k, as in SPDZ more generally, each multiplication consumes one Beaver triple.
Taking the same state-of-the-art figure Fhenix uses for maliciously secure triple generation, Multipars~\cite{CCS:HOSS23} at an amortized $14.9$ kbit per triple per party, the triples for one $\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv2}$ conversion cost roughly $0.53$ MB of communication per party at $N = 4$, against the approximately $4.5$ MB per decryption that Fhenix reports for its own $2478$ multiplications.
For $\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv1}$ and $\Pi_\mathsf{ModConv3}$ this cost is zero.
